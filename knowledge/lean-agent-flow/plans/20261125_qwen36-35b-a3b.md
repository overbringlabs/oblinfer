## Speed budget per token

Measured footprint of the real checkpoint (snapshot `1355db6a`, 124,468 tensors): experts 19.73 GB,
dense 2.35 GB, `lm_head` 0.286 GB (NVFP4), shared expert 0.071 GB, MTP 0.079 GB, vision 0.893 GB.
One expert stride is 1,769,496 B (12 tensors), so a routed layer holds 432 MiB of experts and a token
reads 13.5 MiB of routed weights per layer. Text-only is 22.5 GB, plus 2.68 GB of FP8 KV at 262k.

| Part | Bytes per token | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| Routed experts, top-8 | ~566 MB | 8 × 3 × 2048 × 512 × 0.5625 B, over 40 layers |
| Shared expert | ~71 MB | 1.77 MB × 40 |
| Dense: GDN, attention, lm_head | ~120 MB | FP8 weights |
| FP8 KV at 262k | 2.68 GB per pass | 10 full layers, 2 KV heads, head 256, K and V |

At a combined 1344 GB/s the depth ceiling is ~300 tok/s and shallow depth is compute-bound well above
that. The 60–70% efficiency target is the real goal, measured per context bucket.

## Rules the kernels must follow

1. Weights stay in checkpoint precision in VRAM: FP8 for GDN, attention and `lm_head`; NVFP4 for
   routed and shared experts. No dequantized copies.
2. Experts are packed at load into per-layer contiguous blocks, ordered by expert id, so decode does
   one base pointer per projection and a token's 8 experts are a strided read.
3. Token-major decode: one kernel launch per projection over the 8 routed experts with M=8 rows.
4. FP8 GEMM uses e4m3 tensor cores with per-block scales; `lm_head` is FP8 with bf16 logits.
5. Decode is graph-captured per (width, KV bucket); PDL between dependent kernels; the GPU1 hop
   spliced as child-graph nodes, never event pairs across graph launches.
6. Prefill: chunked GDN delta rule, grouped GEMM over experts, cuBLASLt for dense GEMMs where it wins.
7. Speculation is the checkpoint's own MTP head (19 bf16 tensors), acceptance measured per prompt set.

## Risks

- 16 GB host RAM on supra: no whole-file staging, only per-layer buffers; the loader must stream.
- 122,880 expert tensors in the export: load time is dominated by small reads; parallel per-shard reads
  with fadvise, packed into per-layer pinned staging, then one H2D per block.
- The 27 µs hop is paid twice per visited layer in the current Inkling schedule; the decoder must
  batch per-visit hops the way the Inkling graph does.
- Only 8 KV heads across 10 full layers: the split is 5/3 or 6/2, so KV balance is coarse at depth.
- 23.42 GB of weights plus 2.68 GB of KV leaves ~5 GB on two 16 GB cards; workspace sizes are a
  hard constraint in S6.

Fused MoE is the decoder path; the two-phase router brought 2k depth to 56.6 tok/s and short decode to 73.9
(parity intact). Measured since: the fp8 GEMV reaches **825 GB/s at DRAM size, 97% of the 849 GB/s ceiling**,
so the streaming path is finished; per-token traffic at 2k is ~2.2 GB (dense GDN 1.0, experts 0.57, attention
0.27, head 0.25) and the floor is 2.7 ms against 17.7 actual. The cross-card exchange is **2.72 ms/token (18%)**
with both cards serialised; naive stream overlap reached only 57.8 and raced on the reused pinned buffer, so it
was reverted. The remaining big lever is speculative decoding with the MTP head: one weight stream, several
accepted tokens.

Attribution at 1k depth (15.49 ms/token, `QWEN35MOE_SKIP_*` ablations): routed experts **5.11 ms**, shared
expert ~0, router + cross-card exchange **~4.5 ms**, attention (10 full layers) 2.72 ms, everything else
(dense GDN, norms, head) ~3.1. A single-buffer async exchange reached 13.63 ms (73.3 tok/s, +13%) but could
not be made race-free in this session and was reverted; the win is real and needs double-buffered pinned
staging. The expert path moves 0.57 GB in 5.11 ms (112 GB/s), a 7x gap that points at grid occupancy.

Expert-path occupancy: the fused gate/up stage launched only 64 blocks (64 rows per block), i.e. one block per
SM. Splitting to 8 rows per block gives 512 blocks and took the 1k token from 15.49 to 14.25 ms
(70.2 tok/s), 2k to 60.8, short decode to 81.4, oracle still passing.

Double-buffered async exchange (one pinned slot per direction carrying x + ids + weights out and y back,
event-paired by owner): measured **11.93 ms/token at 1k (83.8 tok/s) and 14.11 ms at 2k (70.9)**, i.e. +19%
over the blocking path. Output degenerates, so it is not landed; the staging hand-off needs a targeted
debug (ids/weights or the y slot). The upside is measured, not hoped for.

KV formats: `KvType { Bf16, Q8_0 }` per side, selected like llama-server's `-ctk`/`-ctv` (interim wiring:
`QWEN35MOE_CTK` / `QWEN35MOE_CT_V`, values `bf16` or `q8_0`). Q8_0 is llama.cpp's block format, 34 bytes per 32
values. Verified: q8_0 matches its reference to 1.3e-6, costs 1.6% vs BF16 at the value level, and produces the
identical 16 greedy ids on the reference prompt. **262144 allocates and runs with K8V8; BF16 at 262144 OOMs**, so
K8V8 is the long-context default and BF16 stays for shorter windows. Drafter caches (`-ctkd`/`-ctvd`) reuse the
same type layer once a drafter exists.

Async exchange landed (double-buffered pinned slot per direction, four paired events, card 1 kernels on a
non-blocking stream): parity intact and the token loop is now enqueue-only, which is the prerequisite for graph
capture. Gains are smaller than the earlier single-buffer measurement: short 81.6 -> 85.4 tok/s, 1k 70.2 ->
71.6, 2k flat at 60.8. The earlier 83.8 tok/s at 1k did not reproduce; treat it as noise until a profile says
otherwise.

**Depth decode does not scale.** K8V8, synthetic-depth probe (`QWEN35MOE_FAKE_DEPTH`, zeroed cache, timing
only): 4k 30.9 tok/s (32 ms), 32k 5.7 (175 ms), 128k 1.5 (665 ms), 258k 0.77 (1,300 ms). Near-linear in depth,
so not a pathology, but ~370x off the 3.5 ms the KV bytes imply. Cause: `decode_attention` launches one block
per query head, i.e. **16 blocks for 262,144 positions** on a 60-SM card, each block walking positions serially
in 512-wide chunks with per-position scalar V reads. The fix is split-K (flash-decoding) attention: grid over
(head, position split), per-split (max, sum, acc), then one combine kernel; and vectorised V reads. Until that
lands, the 258k gate is unreachable regardless of KV format.

**Flash decoding landed** (split-K): grid over (query head, position split), one SplitState per split, a combine
kernel merging them log-sum-exp style. Parity intact (identical 16 ids) and short decode 85.7 tok/s. Depth sweep
with K8V8, synthetic cache: 4k 56.9 tok/s (17.6 ms), 32k 49.9 (20.0), 128k 26.2 (38.2), **258k 15.7 (63.5)** —
20x the previous 0.77 tok/s. Realised KV bandwidth at 258k is ~45 GB/s, so the next 4x comes from vectorised V
reads (4 dims per thread, uint32 of int8s) and a cheaper score path; the llama.cpp bar is 51.7.
