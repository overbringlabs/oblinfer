## Speed budget per token

Measured footprint of the real checkpoint (snapshot `1355db6a`, 124,468 tensors): experts 19.73 GB,
dense 2.35 GB, `lm_head` 0.286 GB (NVFP4), shared expert 0.071 GB, MTP 0.079 GB, vision 0.893 GB.
One expert stride is 1,769,496 B (12 tensors), so a routed layer holds 432 MiB of experts and a token
reads 13.5 MiB of routed weights per layer. Text-only is 22.5 GB, plus 2.68 GB of FP8 KV at 262k.

| Part | Bytes per token | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| Routed experts, top-8 | ~566 MB | 8 × 3 × 2048 × 512 × 0.5625 B, over 40 layers |
| Shared expert | ~71 MB | 1.77 MB × 40 |
| Dense: GDN, attention, lm_head | ~120 MB | FP8 weights |
| FP8 KV at 262k | 2.68 GB per pass | 10 full layers, 2 KV heads, head 256, K and V |

At a combined 1344 GB/s the depth ceiling is ~300 tok/s and shallow depth is compute-bound well above
that. The 60–70% efficiency target is the real goal, measured per context bucket.

## Rules the kernels must follow

1. Weights stay in checkpoint precision in VRAM: FP8 for GDN, attention and `lm_head`; NVFP4 for
   routed and shared experts. No dequantized copies.
2. Experts are packed at load into per-layer contiguous blocks, ordered by expert id, so decode does
   one base pointer per projection and a token's 8 experts are a strided read.
3. Token-major decode: one kernel launch per projection over the 8 routed experts with M=8 rows.
4. FP8 GEMM uses e4m3 tensor cores with per-block scales; `lm_head` is FP8 with bf16 logits.
5. Decode is graph-captured per (width, KV bucket); PDL between dependent kernels; the GPU1 hop
   spliced as child-graph nodes, never event pairs across graph launches.
6. Prefill: chunked GDN delta rule, grouped GEMM over experts, cuBLASLt for dense GEMMs where it wins.
7. Speculation is the checkpoint's own MTP head (19 bf16 tensors), acceptance measured per prompt set.

## Risks

- 16 GB host RAM on supra: no whole-file staging, only per-layer buffers; the loader must stream.
- 122,880 expert tensors in the export: load time is dominated by small reads; parallel per-shard reads
  with fadvise, packed into per-layer pinned staging, then one H2D per block.
- The 27 µs hop is paid twice per visited layer in the current Inkling schedule; the decoder must
  batch per-visit hops the way the Inkling graph does.
- Only 8 KV heads across 10 full layers: the split is 5/3 or 6/2, so KV balance is coarse at depth.
- 23.42 GB of weights plus 2.68 GB of KV leaves ~5 GB on two 16 GB cards; workspace sizes are a
  hard constraint in S6.

Fused MoE is the decoder path; the two-phase router brought 2k depth to 56.6 tok/s and short decode to 73.9
(parity intact). Measured since: the fp8 GEMV reaches **825 GB/s at DRAM size, 97% of the 849 GB/s ceiling**,
so the streaming path is finished; per-token traffic at 2k is ~2.2 GB (dense GDN 1.0, experts 0.57, attention
0.27, head 0.25) and the floor is 2.7 ms against 17.7 actual. The cross-card exchange is **2.72 ms/token (18%)**
with both cards serialised; naive stream overlap reached only 57.8 and raced on the reused pinned buffer, so it
was reverted. The remaining big lever is speculative decoding with the MTP head: one weight stream, several
accepted tokens.

Attribution at 1k depth (15.49 ms/token, `QWEN35MOE_SKIP_*` ablations): routed experts **5.11 ms**, shared
expert ~0, router + cross-card exchange **~4.5 ms**, attention (10 full layers) 2.72 ms, everything else
(dense GDN, norms, head) ~3.1. A single-buffer async exchange reached 13.63 ms (73.3 tok/s, +13%) but could
not be made race-free in this session and was reverted; the win is real and needs double-buffered pinned
staging. The expert path moves 0.57 GB in 5.11 ms (112 GB/s), a 7x gap that points at grid occupancy.

Expert-path occupancy: the fused gate/up stage launched only 64 blocks (64 rows per block), i.e. one block per
SM. Splitting to 8 rows per block gives 512 blocks and took the 1k token from 15.49 to 14.25 ms
(70.2 tok/s), 2k to 60.8, short decode to 81.4, oracle still passing.

Double-buffered async exchange (one pinned slot per direction carrying x + ids + weights out and y back,
event-paired by owner): measured **11.93 ms/token at 1k (83.8 tok/s) and 14.11 ms at 2k (70.9)**, i.e. +19%
over the blocking path. Output degenerates, so it is not landed; the staging hand-off needs a targeted
debug (ids/weights or the y slot). The upside is measured, not hoped for.

KV formats: `KvType { Bf16, Q8_0 }` per side, selected like llama-server's `-ctk`/`-ctv` (interim wiring:
`QWEN35MOE_CTK` / `QWEN35MOE_CT_V`, values `bf16` or `q8_0`). Q8_0 is llama.cpp's block format, 34 bytes per 32
values. Verified: q8_0 matches its reference to 1.3e-6, costs 1.6% vs BF16 at the value level, and produces the
identical 16 greedy ids on the reference prompt. **262144 allocates and runs with K8V8; BF16 at 262144 OOMs**, so
K8V8 is the long-context default and BF16 stays for shorter windows. Drafter caches (`-ctkd`/`-ctvd`) reuse the
same type layer once a drafter exists.

Async exchange landed (double-buffered pinned slot per direction, four paired events, card 1 kernels on a
non-blocking stream): parity intact and the token loop is now enqueue-only, which is the prerequisite for graph
capture. Gains are smaller than the earlier single-buffer measurement: short 81.6 -> 85.4 tok/s, 1k 70.2 ->
71.6, 2k flat at 60.8. The earlier 83.8 tok/s at 1k did not reproduce; treat it as noise until a profile says
otherwise.

**Depth decode does not scale.** K8V8, synthetic-depth probe (`QWEN35MOE_FAKE_DEPTH`, zeroed cache, timing
only): 4k 30.9 tok/s (32 ms), 32k 5.7 (175 ms), 128k 1.5 (665 ms), 258k 0.77 (1,300 ms). Near-linear in depth,
so not a pathology, but ~370x off the 3.5 ms the KV bytes imply. Cause: `decode_attention` launches one block
per query head, i.e. **16 blocks for 262,144 positions** on a 60-SM card, each block walking positions serially
in 512-wide chunks with per-position scalar V reads. The fix is split-K (flash-decoding) attention: grid over
(head, position split), per-split (max, sum, acc), then one combine kernel; and vectorised V reads. Until that
lands, the 258k gate is unreachable regardless of KV format.

**Flash decoding landed** (split-K): grid over (query head, position split), one SplitState per split, a combine
kernel merging them log-sum-exp style. Parity intact (identical 16 ids) and short decode 85.7 tok/s. Depth sweep
with K8V8, synthetic cache: 4k 56.9 tok/s (17.6 ms), 32k 49.9 (20.0), 128k 26.2 (38.2), **258k 15.7 (63.5)** —
20x the previous 0.77 tok/s. Realised KV bandwidth at 258k is ~45 GB/s, so the next 4x comes from vectorised V
reads (4 dims per thread, uint32 of int8s) and a cheaper score path; the llama.cpp bar is 51.7.

Q8_0 cache laid out as two arrays (values, then one __half per 32-value block) instead of an interleaved 34-byte
block: the scale no longer sits at byte offset 2, so the value array is 16-byte aligned and wide reads are legal.
Depth: 32k 49.8 -> 56.1 tok/s, 258k 15.7 -> 19.8. The four-dims-per-thread accumulate was attempted twice and
reverted both times (first misaligned, then slower and wrong when it left 192 of 256 threads idle); the vector
read needs a block layout where the idle threads do the scores.

Flash decode rewritten as warp-per-position with register accumulators (eight dims per lane, online softmax in
registers, eight warps merged through 8 KB of shared memory). No shared score array, no per-chunk barriers.
Depth: 32k 56.1 -> **72.6 tok/s**, 258k 19.8 -> **40.4** (24.7 ms, 115 GB/s of cache read). Parity intact.

Splits A/B: 256 splits of 512 positions gives 32k 74.0 (+2%) and 258k 39.5 (-2%) against 64 splits of 2048
(72.6 / 40.4), so 64/2048 stays: the combine pass grows with the split count and eats the gain at depth.

Orchestration round (three agents): (a) **two warps per position was rejected with measurements** — reduction
cost scales with lanes-per-position, not values-per-lane: 4 dims/lane measured 2.1x slower; the opposite
direction (16 dims/lane) measured 1.17-1.30x faster in a stripped loop, so that is the next attention lever and
needs a production merge of 16 partial streams. (b) **CUDA graph capture works across both cards** via child
graphs: parity exact on sm_120, +2% at 1k/2k, ~1560 ops collapse to one launch; pos is patched per replay (30-40
node updates) until the kernels take a device pos. (c) **Banded KV across cards**: code and a numerical test
delivered, 1.5x on the cache read by construction, but its decoder integration faulted (illegal access surfacing
in the shared expert) and is staged, not landed. The tree is back on the verified commit; staged files are in
/tmp/agent-units/.

### Round two: bands, depth splits, and the verify path (2026-11-26)

**Banded KV is landed and exact** (`kv_bands.cu`, 146 + 358 lines). Each card keeps the position band it serves,
the columns are cut from the *global* `(pos, splits)` grid so no column straddles the cards, and band 1 mirrors
the last rows below the boundary — so every SplitState is bit-identical to the single-cache launch. Cost: 4064
mirrored rows at 262k, 110 MB. Split ratio **2.0**, from three sources: spec bandwidth 896:448, the test's own
probe 330 vs 157 GB/s, and an end-to-end A/B (1.5 → 49.00, 2.0 → 51.99, 2.5 → 51.63). Break-even ≈ 98k, so the
capacity gate defaults to 98304 and bands stay dormant below it.

The staged illegal access was an **initialisation order** bug, not a kernel bug: `kv_band_exchange_init` ends by
restoring the host device, and the caller allocated the device-1 peer buffers while device 0 was current. They
were dereferenced by a kernel on card 1.

**Depth-scaled splits are on by default.** `splits_at(pos)` under the fixed rule returns `(pos + 2048) / 2048`,
which is **1 below 2048 positions** — that falls out of the flash path into the old single-pass kernel and is the
whole cause of the non-monotone depth curve. One column per 128 positions, capped at 64: 1k 68.7 → 83.6 tok/s,
2k 57.3 → 84.1, 4k +3%, flat from 32k up.

Verified defaults now (sm_120, K8V8, synthetic depth, gen 8): 1k 84.3, 2k 83.7, 4k 83.1, 32k 76.1, 98k 65.3,
**258k 51.9** (from 46.2), ids unchanged. Bands and CUDA graphs do not coexist — a banded launch switches the
host thread's device, which a capture may not contain — so the graph is refused while bands are engaged; at 258k
the graph was measured *slower* than eager anyway.

**The verify-width module exists and is tested**: `kernels/batch/*`, 180 checks, ALL PASS, sanitizer clean. Its
honest verdict: a naive 4-token verify is **0.59×** today, because per-position overhead multiplies by four,
batch attention underfills at 57 GB/s, expert dedup converts 38% of bytes into 0% of time, and there is no
rollback. What does win: dense GEMVs 1.82×, attention GEMVs 1.79×, `lm_head` 1.94×, GDN register-staged
recurrence 2.80×. Unique experts for n=4 on real weights: **19.8 of 32** across layers.

**The MTP head is 1.689 GB of BF16**, one full-attention layer, 256 experts top-8, and its experts are
unquantized — 3.4× a quantised trunk layer per draft token. Tensor inventory, shapes, forward order and the
`(1 + w)` norm convention are in `plans/20261126_qwen36-spec-decode.md`. `docs/checkpoints.md` was wrong about
`lm_head` having no `weight_scale_2`: it has one (1.1190e-4) plus `input_scale` (0.0277).

**Placement is backwards, and the arithmetic says it is a small lever.** The uploader splits experts **115 on the
fast card, 141 on the slow one**, because it follows free VRAM, so the 448 GB/s card serves 4.4 of the 8 routed
experts per layer while the 896 GB/s card serves 3.6. Time per layer is then `max(4.4w/448, 3.6w/896)` against
`max(5.35w/896, 2.65w/448)` for a 2:1 split — a factor of **1.64 on the expert read**, not the 3x a total-bytes
argument suggests. One expert is 1.77 MB (NVFP4 plus scales), so the read is 0.57 GB/token, and because the two cards run in
parallel the step waits on the slower one: 4.4 x 1.77 MB / 448 GB/s = 17.4 us per layer, 0.70 ms per token. A 2:1
split gives 10.5 us per layer on both cards, 0.42 ms. **Placement is therefore worth about 0.3 ms of a 14.25 ms
token.** The other ~7 ms of the MoE block is per-layer overhead, which is where the money is.

### Round three: the router was the MoE block (2026-11-26)

The 7.7 ms MoE block was not the fused expert kernel. `router_select_kernel` was launched `<<<1,1>>>`: one thread
ranking 256 experts eight times through a 256-byte local-memory `bool used[]`. nsys measured **95.0 µs per launch,
3.80 ms per token — 49% of the block, 25x the router's own GEMV**. Rewritten as one block of 256 threads with a
rank-based top-k that keeps the same total order, hence bit-identical ids and weights: 2.33 µs per launch.

Runner-up: the shared expert ran five launches of the generic `quant::nvfp4_gemv` (8 rows/block, 1-byte loads),
1.01 ms for 71 MB at ~70 GB/s. Rewritten as gate+up+SiLU in one launch with uint32 loads and the sigmoid-gate
scalar folded into the row scale of the down pass: two launches, 225 GB/s, 0.31 ms. The fused expert kernel also
folds SiLU+mul into the gate/up epilogue, 3 launches to 2.

| router logits | 0.15 | 0.15 |

Also corrected: the isolation bench measured 12 layers, not 40, so the "289 µs/token" that framed the search was

### Round three: Q4_0 KV type (2026-11-26)

`KvType::Q4_0` in the same two-array layout as q8_0 (16 bytes of nibbles per 32 values, scales in a second
array), selectable per side and per cache, with `QWEN35MOE_CTKD` / `QWEN35MOE_CT_VD` plumbed and defaulting to
`q4_0` for the drafter. Trunk default is unchanged at K8V8, per the owner's ruling.

The measured surprise: **cutting K's bytes buys nothing, cutting V's bytes buys a lot.** The split kernel's score
path is latency-bound (dot, butterfly, exp chain), not byte-bound: at 32k, q8_0 K reads 35.7 MB in 108 µs and
q4_0 K reads 18.9 MB in 104 µs. At 258048: K8V8 attention 8.00 ms, K8V4 6.90 ms, K4V4 8.25 ms. So K8V4 is the
useful option (54.99 tok/s against 51.86 for K8V8 at that depth) and Q4_0 K is dead weight until the identity
`sum q*d*(n-8) = d*(sum q*n - 8*sum q)` hoists the offset out of the position loop.

VRAM at 262144: K8V8 2897 MB, K8V4 2215, K4V4 1534 — Q4_0 frees 1.36 GB. Value error: q8_0 5.8e-3 relative,
q4_0 9.6e-2, so four bits cost about 16x q8_0 at the value level.

### Round four: placement policy and the MTP drafter (2026-11-26)

**Placement.** The split now follows the bandwidth share and is clamped by what fits, so supra loads
`136/120` instead of `115/141`: 896/448 GB/s would ask for 171, the fit range is 43..136, and the clamp holds it.
Measured, paired and interleaved at gen 32: **-0.30 ms at 1k (2.5%) and -0.28 ms at 258k (1.5%)**. Small, as the
byte arithmetic predicted, and now honest about why: card 1's *measured* read rate is **162 GB/s, 36% of its
448 GB/s spec**, so 1.63 ms of the 7.49 ms block is card-1 read and **5.86 ms is split-independent** (40 launches,
pinned H4/D2H, per-layer events).

The fit is what binds. At 262,144 the KV bands take 1786 MiB on card 0 and 894 on card 1, the decoder workspace
is 85 MiB (the earlier 0.8 GB estimate was 10x high and cost 12 experts of headroom), leaving 10,721 MiB for
experts on card 0 against 14,385 on card 1 — **35 experts short of the balanced split**. Reaching it needs
2,357 MiB moved off card 0, and the two cheap candidates are the MTP block (1,536 MiB, nothing reads it in plain
decode) and `embed_tokens` (970 MiB, one row per token) — together 2,506 MiB, enough with 149 MiB spare. Moving
dense layer weights instead would need 40 extra hops per token, the same latency class as the hop that already
costs 5.9 ms, to buy 0.46 ms.

**MTP drafter module** (`model/mtp.*`, `kernels/mtp/*`, 63 checks). Loads all 19 tensors, three 64-byte samples
per tensor byte-identical to the file, 1.6893 GB in a 256-byte-aligned block; refuses a directory without the
head. Forward: both `(1 + w)` pre-norms, `fc` with the **embedding half first**, one full-attention layer with
per-head q/k norm, partial rotary and its own Q4_0 split cache, packed BF16 experts (2 launches), shared expert,
`mtp.norm`, the trunk's NVFP4 head. The test carries an **FP64 host implementation of the whole block** that reads
the checkpoint's own bytes: agreement 1.2e-6..3.1e-6 on `h'` and 1.4e-6..2.0e-6 on the 248,320 logits, draft token
and router top-8 identical at all seven positions. A swapped concat or a wrong norm convention shows at 1e-1.

Cost per drafted token on card 0: **0.879 ms at 1k, 0.947 at 32k, 1.739 at 262,144**, of which the trunk head is
0.605-0.607 and the drafter's own attention grows to 0.916 at depth (165 GB/s on 8-byte lane loads — the one
stage with headroom). Bytes: 416/434/566 MB. That is 3.2x a quantized trunk layer, matching the BF16 experts.
VRAM: weights 1.6893 GB + q4_0 cache 0.1510 GB at 262,144 + 2.2 MB scratch = 1.8425 GB, inside the 1.84 the
budget reserved.
